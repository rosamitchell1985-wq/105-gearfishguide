Beach Fishing for Beginners: The Complete Australian Guide

By Ron Callahan · 28 September 2026 · 8 min read

Beach fishing is the most accessible fishing in Australia. No boat, no expensive gear, no special licence endorsements in most states — just you, the sand, and some of the best table fish in the world. Here's everything you need for your first day, and nothing you don't.

The gear you actually need

Ignore the shops that want to sell you a A$600 setup. For Australian beach fishing you need four things:

Reading the beach (the 2-minute lesson)

Fish on a beach gather in the deeper channels where the waves break last. Look along the shoreline for:

  • A deeper, darker patch of water — usually a channel between two sand bars
  • A break in the waves — where waves don't crest, the water is deeper
  • White water edges — fish hold just off the foam line where food gets stirred up

Cast into the channel, let your sinker settle, and wait. Beach fishing is 90% patience and 10% panic.

Tides and timing

The two hours either side of a rising tide is prime time — fish move in with the incoming water to feed. Dawn and dusk double your chances. Free tide apps like WillyWeather or the Bureau of Meteorology marine forecast will tell you exactly when to go.

Beach safety for seniors

  • Always wear a hat, sunglasses and SPF 50+ — the Australian sun off the water is fierce even in winter.
  • Fish with a mate, or at least tell someone where you're going and when you'll be back.
  • Keep an eye on the swell; never turn your back on the ocean for long.
  • Check your state fisheries app for bag and size limits before you keep a fish.
  • A cheap rod holder in the sand saves your wrists — set the rod, sit back, and watch the tip.

What you might catch

Depending on your state: flathead (everywhere, great eating), whiting (superb on the plate), Australian salmon (a genuine fight on light gear), bream, dart and, if you're very lucky, a mulloway — the fish of a lifetime off any beach.

Your first-day checklist

  • Fishing licence (check your state — seniors are often discounted or free)
  • Rod, rig, bait, sinkers and spare hooks
  • Rod holder, chair, hat, water and a bucket
  • Rising tide, dawn or dusk, a deep channel
  • Measuring tape and your phone with the fisheries app open

That's the whole game. Everything else is refinement. See you on the sand.
